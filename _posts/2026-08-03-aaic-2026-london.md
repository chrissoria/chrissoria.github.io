On July 12 I presented at the Gateway/HCAP consensus session at the Alzheimer's Association International Conference (AAIC) in London, sharing work from the [Caribbean American Dementia and Aging Study (CADAS)](/publication/2025-08-29-cadas-protocol) on a multinational framework for online cognitive impairment diagnosis.

The short version: twenty-seven clinicians from five countries (the Dominican Republic, Mexico, Puerto Rico, the United States, and India) independently classified 300 older adults using CADAS survey data, applying a structured 10-question NIA-AA diagnostic algorithm through a web-based consensus platform. Each case was reviewed by at least three clinicians per country team, cases with within-country disagreement went to moderated consensus discussion, and international consensus was defined by majority rule across all five teams. The figure above gives a taste of the results: the country teams classified dementia at noticeably different rates, and their disagreement with the international consensus ranged from 2% (United States) to 10% (India). Getting clinicians on three continents to review the same evidence through one platform is as much a coordination problem as a clinical one, and seeing it work end to end was the most satisfying part of the project.

A huge thank you to all of my coauthors for their contributions, especially [William Dow](https://sph.berkeley.edu/william-h-dow), [Jorge Llibre-Guerra](https://neurology.wustl.edu/people/jorge-llibre-guerra-md/), and [Jinkook Lee](https://dornsife.usc.edu/cesr/profile/jinkook-lee/) of the [Gateway to Global Aging](https://g2aging.org/) project.

It was a pleasure meeting everyone there. London itself was a highlight (the whole city is like a museum), and I'm looking forward to presenting at AAIC again someday.
